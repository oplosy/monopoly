# Deal City: painted scene and ambient life (design, Plan 9)

**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Status:** Approved in conversation; written spec awaiting the user's review.
**Parent spec:** `2026-09-24-table-redesign-design.md` (the picnic table redesign, Plans 6–8). This spec is an addendum: where the two disagree, this one wins for the sections it names in §9. Everything else in the parent stands, including D1–D13.

---

## 1. Why

The user made a 10-second concept video: an AI image-to-video of a screenshot of our table. It turned the flat CSS picnic into a painted, sunlit scene that is quietly alive: leaves sway in the foreground, dappled light drifts over the table, the lake shimmers and a butterfly lands on a bowl. The user wants the game screen to look and feel like that. The cards, avatars and HUD stay flat vector, exactly as the video shows them.

The video itself cannot ship: the table and the UI are baked into it, its text is garbled, it cannot loop seamlessly, and it would not line up with the 3D table plane. So the scene is rebuilt from separate painted layers, and the motion is made in code.

### 1.1 Success criteria

- At a glance the table screen reads like the concept video: a painted meadow, a lake with a pier, a real wooden round table with a thick rim, a gingham cloth, painted dishes, foreground leaves.
- The scene is quietly alive (§5) and never distracts from play: no ambient layer ever covers a card, a seat's UI, the hand or the HUD, and none of them ever takes a click.
- Gameplay, layout, flights, sounds and every existing test are unchanged.
- 60 fps holds on a mid-range laptop during a 3-player game (parent §9.4). Ambient motion runs on the compositor only (transform and opacity).
- Reduced motion shows a still picture: no ambient animation at all, no butterfly.
- Art weighs under 1.2 MB on a desktop and less on a phone; the ambience loop about 250 KB more, loaded only once sound is unlocked.

## 3. The scene: layers, back to front

This replaces parent §4.1 items 1 and 2 (ground and table plane). Items 3 and 4 (flat UI, effects) are unchanged.

1. **Ground plate (screen space).** A box with the plate's aspect ratio (3:2 landscape, 2:3 portrait), sized to *cover* the scene and centered, holding:
   - the painted plate (`bg-landscape.webp`, or `bg-portrait.webp` when the scene is taller than it is wide), as a `<picture>` so the browser downloads only the one it shows;
   - the water shimmer (§5.3), positioned in the plate's own coordinates, so it stays on the lake however the plate is cropped.

   Until the plate loads, today's grass gradient shows through (it stays as the fallback background). The vignette stays on top of the plate.
2. **Table plane (the 55° plane, parent §4.1).**
   - **Tabletop:** `table-top.webp` fills the plane's disk, replacing today's CSS wood gradient. It keeps today's soft drop shadow on the grass.
   - **Thick rim:** two or three copies of the disk, darkened and moved a few pixels down in Z under the tabletop, read as the table's edge in perspective. No extra art.
   - **Cloth:** `cloth.webp`, on the left part of the table at a slight rotation, where today's CSS cloth lies. It keeps a soft shadow.
   - **Light:** `dapple.webp`, tiled, blended with `multiply` at a low opacity, clipped to the disk, *beneath* every card (card faces stay clean and readable).
   - **Dishes:** the three painted dishes, placed by `propLayout` on the rim between seats, never on play zones (parent §4.5 rule kept). Their kinds become `melon`, `chips` and `berries`.
   - **Cards** and anchors, unchanged.
   - **Butterfly** (§5.4), above the dishes.
3. **Foreground leaves (screen space).** `leaves-left.webp` hangs in from the left edge and `leaves-top.webp` from the top edge, softly out of focus. They sit above the table plane and *below* the flat UI layer, so avatars, name ribbons, the hand, trays and the HUD are always in front of them. `pointer-events: none`. On a portrait phone they are smaller, so they never cover more than a thin strip of the edges.
4. **Flat UI** and **effects**, unchanged.

The `backdrop` variant (behind the paper pages) shows the plate and the table (with its blur and slow drift, as today), without leaves, shimmer or butterfly.

## 5. Ambient life

A new motion layer, "layer 0", under parent §6. It is always quiet: slow, small and never in the way. All of it uses transform and opacity only.

**Where it runs:** at the game table and in the lobby (the `table` variant of the scene). Not behind the paper pages.

**Reduced motion:** none of it runs. The leaves, light and water are still, and the butterfly never appears. A unit test extends the existing `motion-css` rule (no animation under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`) to the scene's CSS.

### 5.1 Leaves

Each leaf layer rotates by 1–3° around the point where its branch enters the screen, with `ease-in-out` and `alternate`. The two layers use different durations (about 7 s and 9 s) and start out of phase, so the movement never looks mechanical. Slight scale breathing (≤ 1 %) is allowed.

### 5.2 Dappled light

The tiled light texture drifts slowly across the table: a 40–60 s `alternate` loop of `translate` on a layer larger than the disk, which stays clipped to the disk. It replaces today's `dapple-drift`.

### 5.3 Water shimmer

- A caustics texture (bright, soft, web-like ridges on black, seamlessly tileable) is generated once in code during the plan and committed as `caustics.webp`. It needs no extra art from the user.
- Two copies slide in opposite directions at different speeds (about 20 s and 27 s loops), blended with `screen` at a low opacity.
- They are clipped to the lake by a polygon mask, one per plate (landscape and portrait), in the plate's own percent coordinates. The polygons are traced from the plates during the plan and stored in code (`scene/lake.ts`).

### 5.4 The butterfly

- **When:** the first visit comes 10–20 s after the scene mounts; later ones every 30–60 s, at random.
- **Path:** it flies in from beyond one side of the table, follows a gentle curve **along the rim** (never across the play zones inside the rim; in plane terms its distance from the center stays at 0.75 R or more), and lands on one of the dishes. It rests there for 3–6 s, flapping slowly now and then, then flies off beyond another side. The flight takes 3–5 s each way.
- **Flapping:** the image is split into two halves (left and right wing) with `clip-path`; each half rotates around the body on the Y axis. Fast while flying, slow and occasional while resting.
- **Code:** a pure function builds a visit from a random source: the path's keyframes (in plane percent), the dish it lands on and the timings. It is unit-tested with a seeded random source (it lands on a dish; every point stays at 0.75 R or more from the center, or off the table). The flight runs with the Web Animations API (compositor), like Plan 7's flights.
- **Never:** when the tab is hidden (the pending visit waits until the page is visible again), under reduced motion, where the Web Animations API is missing (jsdom), or in the backdrop variant. It never takes a click.

## 6. Ambient sound

This extends parent §8.

- **The file:** one recorded CC0 loop of a meadow by a lake (birds, lapping water, a little wind), from a source that needs no account (for example OpenGameArt, filtered to CC0). It is trimmed to 30–60 s, made mono, and its ends are cross-faded so the loop point is inaudible. It is saved as `apps/web/public/ambience/meadow.ogg` and `.mp3` (about 48 kbps, together about 250 KB). **The user listens to the prepared loop before it is committed.** `CREDITS.md` names the source, the author and the license.
- **Playing:** the audio manager gains an ambience channel: `setAmbience(on: boolean)`.
  - Turning it on fetches and decodes the loop (once), then plays it as a looping buffer source through its own gain, fading in over 2 s. Turning it off fades out over 1 s and stops the source.
  - Its level is 0.25 of the master volume, and it follows the master volume and mute. Nothing plays until sound is unlocked by a gesture (Plan 8 rules). A missing or undecodable file is silent.
  - While the tab is hidden, the ambience fades out; it fades back in when the page is visible again (parent §8: a hidden tab hears only the turn chime).
- **Where:** the game table and the lobby turn it on while mounted, and off when they unmount.
- **No separate toggle** for now: the HUD's Sound button and Volume slider control it with everything else. A separate ambience switch is a later option if players ask for it.
- The `/gallery` sound board gets an "Ambience" button that starts and stops it, for the listening review.
- A unit test checks the ambience files exist, next to `sound-files.test.ts`; that test's rule that `public/sounds/` holds only the samples is unaffected, because the loop lives in `public/ambience/`.

## 7. Performance and loading

- The plate and tabletop load with the first page that shows the scene (the home page's backdrop), so they are cached before the game opens. The other layers load when the table or lobby scene mounts.
- Images are `decoding="async"`; nothing blocks the first paint, and the CSS fallback is visible until they arrive.
- The animated layers get `will-change: transform` and are their own compositor layers; no ambient animation touches layout or paint.
- The butterfly is the only JavaScript, and it runs one WAAPI animation per visit, not per frame.
- The parent budget stands: 60 fps on a mid-range laptop during a 3-player game, with flights and the ambient layer running together.

## 8. Testing

- **Unit (Vitest):**
  - the art manifest: exactly the files of §3.1, both download budgets;
  - the ambience files exist;
  - `propLayout` with the new kinds (the existing geometry tests, updated);
  - the butterfly's visit builder, with a seeded random source;
  - the ambience channel of the audio manager with the fake context the Plan 8 manager tests use: fade in and out, follows mute and volume, silent before unlock, fades out while hidden;
  - the reduced-motion CSS rule extended to the scene's CSS;
  - the butterfly never mounts under reduced motion or in the backdrop.
- **End to end (Playwright, Chrome):**
  - the ambient layers never take a click: a point over the leaves hits the table or the UI beneath them;
  - with reduced motion, no ambient animation is running (`document.getAnimations()`);
  - every existing spec stays green.
- **Visual review:** screenshots at 1440×900, 768×1024, 375×812 and 812×375, and a CDP screencast of the ambient motion and a butterfly visit. The user looks at them before the PR.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Painted plate and CSS-3D table look mismatched in angle or light | The plates were painted from a high angle matching the 55° tilt, with light from the upper left like the tabletop; the visual review checks it, and the tilt may be tuned a few degrees (a ruling, not a new decision). |
| Leaves cover a seat on narrow screens | They sit below the flat UI layer by design, and shrink on portrait phones; the visual review checks each viewport. |
| Art weight on phones | The portrait plate replaces the landscape one; WebP; a unit test holds the budget. |
| The ambience loop is annoying | Low level (0.25 of master), a listening review before commit, and the master mute switches it off. |
| No suitable CC0 loop without an account | Fall back to a synthesized ambience (filtered noise for water and wind, no birds) and tell the user; that is a ruling in the plan. |
| Commercial terms of the generated art | OpenAI's terms assign the output to the user; the user checks them if the game goes commercial. |
